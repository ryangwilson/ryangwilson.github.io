overview: I joined the Yum! team as a consultant in the role of Product Owner and UX Consultant for an enterprise menu management tool (MMT). The MMT's purpose is to create products, pricing, taxes, store information, combo deals, and more for the Yum! Brands Taco Bell, KFC, and Pizza Hut.<br> <br>Each of the brands, Taco Bell, Pizza Hut, and KFC had its own legacy MMTs, glossaries, and processes for updating data. It was essential to observe and research how each brand was accomplishing tasks, listen to users' and multiple stakeholders' needs, and recommend solutions that work across all brands.
goals: The MMT would be a place for users to create and input all details necessary for a fully functional store and would eventually be a single-source product for many different user types. Each user type would have its purposes and tasks within the product. All of the data within the MMT would feed into brand applications and eCommerce solutions like KFC.com to supply product details, store hours, product information, and pricing by location. This data would flow to stores for back-of-house and point of sale systems.
role: Product Owner & UX Consultant
scope: Two years
tools: Research, Observation, Workshops, Documentation, Presentation, Usability Testing, Sketching, Figma, Axure, Zeplin
problemstatement: Create a single tool to manage all of the data that make up products, menus, discounts, pricing, taxes, store information, customer support, etc. for all US KFC, Pizza Hut, and Taco Bell locations. In later phases, the MMT will be rolled out internationally.
methods: <ul><li>Created and maintained a source of truth repository</li><li>Captured a cross-brand project glossary</li><li>Hosted workshops with stakeholders and brand representatives</li><li>Performed cross-brand user observations</li><li>Developed personas</li><li>Wrote scenarios and usability testing plans</li><li>Wrote user stories</li><li>Created heuristics and usability review reporting</li><li>Developed the initial design system and implemented patterns</li><li>Helped with scrum, sprint planning, and establishing timelines</li><li>Communicated the direction of the product</li><li>Outlined requirements for features, interactions, and refinements</li><li>Vetted concepts with developers</li><li>Planned and performed usability testing</li><li>Supported onboarding of new team members</li></ul>
process: As with every project, I started with a repository to hold the documentation, then worked to understand what I was actually consolidating&#58; four separate, brand-specific MMTs, each with its own workflow and vocabulary. I observed Data Managers at each brand step through their process, which surfaced the pain points and inconsistencies a single tool would need to resolve.<br> <br>That research became a persona deck representing the product's range of users, and from there, user stories, refined product flows, and tickets groomed with the business analyst, all vetted with developers to align on when and how each piece would get built.
findings: Cross-brand workshops with stakeholders from Yum! and all three brands became the mechanism for aligning timelines and requirements, since defining a single product meant accounting for every brand's use cases without shortchanging any of them.<br> <br>Keeping that alignment across pods meant surfacing gaps early and helping shape sprint planning, working with the team, stakeholders, and other product owners to set feature timelines and outline requirements as they came into focus.<br> <br>On the interface side, I built common UI patterns in Material UI and turned them into wireframes developers could build from directly, then closed the loop with usability testing and QA to confirm what shipped matched what was designed.
processlist: <ul><li>Product Flows</li><li>Sketches</li><li>Wireframes</li><li>Design System</li><li>Mockups</li><li>Prototypes</li><li>Usability Testing</li><li>Iteration & Refinement</li></ul>
solutions: Many of the MMT features I designed are in production across all three brands, simplifying a previously fragmented process, reducing onboarding time for new team members, and unifying how each brand manages its data. The product continues to scale with the needs of stakeholders and users.
outcomes: <ul><li>Defined feature timelines for the MMT product with stakeholders across all three brands</li><li>Created documentation to live as a central source of truth and support project onboarding</li><li>Defined initial interface patterns for the MMT product</li><li>Assisted in sprint planning, writing user stories, and pulling together resources for issues</li><li>Worked alongside stakeholders, BA’s, Tech Leads, POs, and Developers to define and align project needs</li><li>Researched, ideated, and iterated through features with stakeholders, vetted with developers and users</li><li>Developed concepts for the complex Tax Logic Builder feature</li><li>Helped build and mentor Yum.dev’s UX team</li><li>Grew the engagement into a lasting, multi-year relationship with Yum!</li></ul>
achievements: Getting Taco Bell, KFC, and Pizza Hut to agree on a shared language and process for maintaining menus, pricing, and store data, work that continues to run and grow today, was the core of this engagement.<br> <br><ul><li>Helped imagine a single-source product for all brands to update menus, products, deals, coupons, store details, and depletion information</li><li>Defined and improved product flows through the system</li><li>Helped create a single location for cross-brand customer support</li></ul>
takeaways: The UI ran on Material UI, per developer preference. It's a fine library, but I think it limited our pattern design. If I could do this again, I'd push for Tailwind instead, for more flexibility as the patterns and design elements grew over time.
learnings: This project reinforced something I later wrote about&#58; <a href="https://medium.com/@ryangwilson/being-comfortable-with-ambiguity-8a98744fdb2f">the value of staying comfortable with ambiguity</a>. It was massive, with a lot of information to capture, understand, and organize, and the connections between concepts weren't always apparent in the short term.<br> <br>In my career, I've frequently had stakeholders tell me their product "was for everyone." The Yum! MMT was the first time I saw that actually be true. Understanding every potential user type, the tasks they'd perform, and the interaction patterns available in each scenario mattered, and having an organized design system was what let the team align on solutions.
